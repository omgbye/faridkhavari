Step 4 · Inside the price

Why does an egg cost what it costs?

Tap an item and its price splits open. Energy, transport and interest light up — because those are the costs hiding inside almost everything else, and they're the ones Khavari's plan goes after.

Khavari's point: costs are hidden inside other costs. Make energy nearly free and money nearly interest-free, and food gets cheaper — without anyone being paid less. Khavari's proposal

Assumptions & sources — every rate, share and default
  • Price growth (today's economy), per year: home/rent 3%, electricity 4%, gasoline 3%, car payment 0% (a fixed loan), groceries 3%, insurance 6%, health care 6%, education 5%, credit interest 0% (a fixed balance), property tax 3%, everything else 2.5%. Illustrative defaults in the ranges of recent BLS Consumer Price Index category movements and EIA energy price trends — check the live BLS CPI and EIA series for current values. Lab estimate
  • Mortgage math: standard amortization, payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n). Default loan: 6.5% over 30 years. The public-bank switch refinances the same balance at 3% over the same remaining term; the 0% switch pays the balance off over 10 years (payment = balance / 120 months), then $0 — his "paid off in 10 years instead of 30" example. Khavari's proposal
  • Solar: a $18,000 rooftop system financed at 0% over 12 years ($125/month) plus $25/month maintenance; after payoff, maintenance only, with a "PAID OFF — yours" ribbon. The electric switch replaces gasoline with a $60/month charging cost ($25 with solar on the roof). Illustrative figures. Lab estimate
  • Groceries: new price = price × (1 − energy share × 80% − transport share × 70% − interest share × 90%) when the energy/transport switches are on; local food self-sufficiency takes a further 7% off. Segment shares are approximate, in the pattern of USDA food-dollar breakdowns, and labeled approximate wherever shown. Lab estimate
  • Insurance: the universal-policy switch reduces combined premiums by 35% (illustrative, adjustable in his full plan) to a single policy covering health, car, life, disaster, theft and flood — and halves out-of-pocket health-care costs, since the policy is what pays the hospital. Both figures are illustrative. Khavari's proposal
  • Wealth Jar: wealth(t) = the sum of monthly leftovers × 12, with no investment returns — deliberately conservative. Retirement age: the first age at which the Wealth Jar could cover all remaining costs from that age to 90, in that economy. Freedom year: the first year costs fall below income and stay there.
  • Economic Security Score (0–100): 40% × min(1, months of expenses saved ÷ 24) + 30% × share of income left over (capped at 50%) + 20% × share of essential costs fully owned or paid off + 10% × (1 − debt-to-income, floor 0). An illustration of his Economic Security Indices idea — not his formula. Lab estimate
  • Honesty rule: anything labeled "Khavari's proposal" is his policy or claim, from Towards a Zero-Cost Economy (2009) and zerocosteconomy.com. Anything labeled "Lab estimate" is this tool's arithmetic on illustrative defaults. Neither is a forecast.
