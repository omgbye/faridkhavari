About the Author

Back cover of Zero Cost Economics (ISBN 979-8-9959856-1-7) — the biography on this page is our own retelling of it, in new words.

A life, in chapters — tap a year

Farid A. Khavari, Ph.D., is an economist and inventor whose work has never fit comfortably inside the textbooks. Where conventional economics accepts debt, inflation, and ever-rising costs as the price of growth, he has spent over fifty years arguing the opposite: strip out the costs a society doesn't actually need, and prosperity follows — without printing money, and without leaving anyone behind.

He didn't come to that idea in a library. Born in Yazd, Iran, he grew up in India, where his family helped build Bahá'í communities and his father played a key role in acquiring the land where New Delhi's Lotus Temple now stands. As a small boy he was taken to see Gandhi. At nineteen, in the first class of Iran's Education Corps, he was sent to a village in the north to teach sixty children and adults — and stayed to help them build a school, find clean water, and break free of a lender charging 100% interest. It's not hard to see where an economist is born.

Germany gave him the credentials: a master's at Hamburg, a doctorate at Bremen in 1976, research posts, and an editor's chair covering raw materials. America gave him the laboratory. Arriving in 1977, he founded a solar company in Miami the following year — designing, manufacturing, and installing systems, earning patents along the way — because energy that pays for itself, then keeps paying you, is his whole philosophy in hardware form.

His earlier books — Environomics, The Final Crash, and Towards a Zero Cost Economy — warned about crises that later arrived, and offered calmer, more humane fixes while there was still time to use them. The new Zero Cost Economics series is his most complete statement yet: a debt-free, non-inflationary design that swaps greed for ethics, scarcity for ingenuity, and poverty for prosperity shared by everyone.

He lives in Florida with his wife, Janilla. They have two children. He speaks English, German, Farsi, and Dari — and, at 83, he is still publishing.

History

A chapter in his history: the campaigns

Dr. Khavari is not running for office today. The campaigns below are part of his biography — the years he took his economic plan directly to the voters.

His 2010 campaign poster — "Economic Security for All Floridians."
"When the house is on fire, you don't call the plumber — you call the firefighter. When the economy is failing, you don't call a politician. You call an economist."

Announcing in 2009, Khavari made a straightforward case: Florida had enormous economic potential and enormous economic problems, and the people who helped create the mess weren't going to fix it. His plan proposed using ordinary banking rules through a new Bank of the State of Florida — earning billions a year for the state treasury, saving homeowners thousands a year, covering the state's investment losses, and then cutting taxes. No higher taxes. That was the whole pitch, and he took it around the state as an independent candidate for Governor in 2010 and again in 2014.

In 2016 he ran for Miami-Dade County Mayor, bringing the same lens home: sea-level rise, the Turkey Point nuclear plant, and the future of the Biscayne Aquifer. The campaigns ended, the argument didn't — it became the books above, and the promise in his old slogan, Economic Security for All Floridians, is still the promise that runs through every one of them.
